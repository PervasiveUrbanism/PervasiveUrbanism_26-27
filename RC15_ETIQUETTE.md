# RC15 Etiquette

## Introduction

This document brings together advice developed through many years of teaching RC15. Over time, we have encountered the same problems and challenges repeatedly—and often found ourselves giving the same advice.

We have therefore compiled these points into a single document, which will be discussed with the whole group at the beginning of each academic year. It sets out a few practical principles intended to support effective collaboration, a positive studio culture, and a successful year for everyone.

## Work together in person

Working from home is strongly discouraged. Come to Gordon Street and work alongside your group whenever possible.

Past years have consistently shown that groups who meet and work together physically produce stronger work, collaborate more effectively, and resolve problems more quickly. The studio is also a place for peer learning: you are expected to share knowledge, exchange ideas, and learn by observing how others work.

Make a conscious effort to avoid falling into a remote-working routine. Being physically present and contributing to the shared studio culture is an important part of the course.

## Working in groups

We strongly encourage you to work in groups of three to four people. Group work allows projects to achieve greater depth and breadth than would normally be possible individually.

Collaboration and communication are essential skills to develop. Architecture is rarely produced in isolation; it emerges through discussion, negotiation, and the exchange of ideas. Developing and delivering a project together is therefore an important part of the peer-learning experience and valuable preparation for your future career.

## Always include both tutors

All email correspondence concerning RC15 must be addressed to both tutors. Always include both tutors—either as recipients or by copying the second tutor into the email.

This rule applies without exception, even if the matter appears to concern only one tutor. It makes our lives considerably easier, keeps us both informed, and prevents emails from falling through the gaps.

## End-of-year show

The end-of-year show takes place in the autumn. Final portfolio submissions are typically due at the end of August, while September is reserved for preparing and installing the exhibition.

Attendance during the exhibition setup and the show is mandatory. You must therefore ensure that you remain in London and are available until the end of September. Please take this into account when planning travel, holidays, or your return home.

## Data and backup

Almost every year, at least one student experiences a computer failure resulting in work being lost. Computers can also be stolen, damaged, or simply fall from a table.

You must therefore keep your work continuously backed up using a reliable cloud-storage service such as Dropbox, Google Drive, OneDrive, or a similar platform. Do not rely solely on files stored locally on your computer or on a single external drive.

You are responsible for safeguarding your own work. Hardware failure, loss, theft, or damaged files are not valid reasons for missing a submission deadline. If you are unable to submit because your work was not adequately backed up, it will be treated as a non-submission.
