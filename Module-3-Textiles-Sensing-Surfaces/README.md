# Textiles — Sensing surfaces

[Programme schedule](../README.md#dates) · [Module description](../README.md#skills-module-3-textile-workshop---sensing-surfaces)

## Materials

Arantza will give guidance on fabric choices at the end of the first session.

### Session 1: Draping on the body

- **3 m of material**, at least **1 m wide**, ideally with some body: a roll of very wide paper, carpet underlay, wide bubble wrap, dust sheets, or similar materials available from hardware and DIY stores such as B&Q or Wickes. **Do not bring fabric for this exercise.**
- Bulldog clips, paper clips, a staple gun, or masking tape for securing the material.
- Scissors or a scalpel.

### Session 1: Folding and pleating

- **A4 heavyweight cartridge paper (100 gsm)**: approximately **6–10 sheets per person**. Students can purchase a pack together.
- Suggested paper: [Fabriano Accademia A4 drawing paper, 160 gsm, 150 sheets](https://www.jacksonsart.com/fabriano-accademia-drawing-paper-a4-160gsm-150s). The linked pack is 160 gsm, while the materials list specifies 100 gsm.
- Cutting mat.
- Pencil.
- Ruler, at least **30 cm** long.
- Knife or scalpel.
- Masking tape.

### Session 2

Bring the materials above, plus:

- Fabric (see guidance below).
- Fabric scissors.
- String, rubber bands, or bulldog clips.
- Safety pins.
- Tin foil.

### Fabric choices for Session 2

To create three-dimensional forms in textiles, work with **synthetic fibres such as polyester**. Inexpensive fabrics can hold folds and three-dimensional shapes well. You may want to try a variety of weights and drapes; as a general rule, choose fabrics with some stiffness rather than very drapey fabrics.

Suitable fabrics include:

- Polyester linings.
- Polyester organza.
- Synthetic felts.
- Polyester satins.
- Taffetas made from synthetic fibres.
